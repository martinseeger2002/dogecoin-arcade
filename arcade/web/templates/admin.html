Chains

Every coin this arcade knows, with how many linked arcades share it. Turn on as many as you like: run a chain's Core on this machine, or borrow it from an arcade that shares its Core through the mesh. Pepecoin testnet always runs here, and at least one more chain must too. Share (on unless you untick it) lets other arcades use a Core that runs here: they can read blocks and the mempool and send transactions people signed, never touch this node's wallet.

Same index as other arcades?

Every arcade builds its index from the chain alone, so they should all hold the same tokens, inscriptions, names and trades. Linked arcades compare fingerprints of their indexes every minute; a difference is tracked down to the block it starts at.

Mine test coins

This machine's CPU mines blocks on a test-coin chain, with work from its Core: the one here, or the one it borrows. On a borrowed chain a tenth of every block goes to the arcade that shares the Core, in the block itself. A testnet lets anyone mine its next block at the lowest difficulty when none has been found for a while, which is when a CPU wins.

What this node takes of a trade it made the offer for (a sale from your shop, a fill of your order, an offer you accepted), paid by the buyer on top of the price and named in the offer. The same rate is held in every lock-script swap lock made here, on top of what is traded, and paid to this node's own address on that chain when the other side claims it (at least that chain's dust line; a token or NFT lock pays the line alone unless its price is coin on the same chain). Off asks nothing of anybody.

{# Off by default (2026-10-13): trades only while both people's tabs are open, signed in each browser; on, this node also finishes trades for people who left. #}

Off (the default): every trade happens while both people have their tab open, built and signed in their own browsers, so this node is trusted with nothing. On: this node also holds pre-signed listings and bids to finish later, fills “while I'm away” buy orders, and may run the custodial mainnet PEP swap below — convenient, but people then trust this node to finish their trades. Lock-script swaps between chains are trustless either way (see Lock-script swaps). It is about clients coming through the web portal: this node's own swaps (listing and taking orders from its wallet) work whichever way it is set.

Mainnet PEP swap

Trade testnet assets (tokens, testnet coins, NFTs) for Pepecoin on mainnet. This node holds both sides while an order stands and pays them out itself, taking 0.5% of the PEP of each trade. Not trustless: every page that offers it tells people they are trusting you, the operator.

Keep 1 to 2 PEP of your own in the mainnet wallet for network fees: a payout waits rather than spend what is held for somebody else. Saved with Save switches.

Lock-script swaps

Swaps between two chains (PEP for DOGE and the like), settled by lock scripts: trustless -- nobody holds anybody's coins. People's tabs hand this node their own pre-signed claims and refunds so a swap finishes with the tab closed; it can only send those, paying the person who signed them. Content screening

A model (vLLM, or any OpenAI-compatible server that reads images) looks at pictures and posts before this arcade shows them. Leave the address empty and nothing is screened: everything shows.

{# 2026-10-17: what this arcade's model flags, told to every arcade (arcade/flags.py). #}

Each picture or post your model flags goes out as a public message every arcade reads: only its fingerprint and the verdict, never the content. Flags are gathered and sent together, a testnet transaction at a time, from this node’s fee address. Other operators choose whether to use them.

Game cards

A new game card that looks like somebody else’s game (a lookalike name, the same cover) is hidden from the Games tab until you look at it, and you get a notification. Your screening model judges the close ones; without one, a close lookalike is hidden on its own. The first to publish a name is taken as the real one, and trusted publishers are never hidden.

Nothing hidden.

Sensitive-material flags from other arcades

Arcades that broadcast what their own screening flagged. Turn one on and what it flags is covered here too (sensitive) or hidden (illegal), even with your own screening off. Their flags can only make this arcade stricter, and anything you clear yourself stays cleared.

No arcade has broadcast flags yet.

Call relay

Video calls and fast games connect players’ browsers directly. Some homes (Starlink, mobile data) cannot be reached that way; a relay carries their calls instead. Make a TURN key in Cloudflare (Realtime → TURN Server → Create), then paste its id and API token here. Signed-in players get short-lived logins to it; strangers do not. Cloudflare bills what it carries past its free allowance.
