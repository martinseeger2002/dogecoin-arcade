Inscribe one thing

One piece — a little under 7,500 bytes — in one transaction, paid for by this account and signed in this tab, so this node never sees the key that spent it. It cannot be taken back afterwards. A bigger file does not fit in one transaction, and the pieces of one file chain onto each other, so it is not something this account can put up at all: split it, or make it smaller.

Run a collection

A folder laid out the way the Art Engine leaves one — json/_metadata.json and the pictures. Each item goes up in its own transaction, signed here, one at a time: this tab stays open while the run is going, and if it closes the run is still there when you come back, at the piece it stopped on. Your node paces it by the inscriptions it will take in an hour, and it keeps the pictures you uploaded — nothing throws them away yet.

Put a price on it

Nothing moves and nothing is held: the piece stays where it is and the price is honoured only while you still hold it. Anybody's node can read it, so it appears in the Exchange and in its collection.

Sell it without you

A price put on the chain says what you want for the piece. Getting paid for it still needs your signature, and this node cannot supply one — it has never seen your key. This is the other kind of promise: this tab signs the bargain now, the piece against that price with your change back to this address, and hands this node the signature. A buyer then completes it without asking you, at the price the signature says, and a signature sent to a server cannot be called back. Spending the piece is the way out: any spend of it ends the listing, because there is nothing left to give. Listing the same piece twice puts two prices up beside each other and both stand until a block spends it.
